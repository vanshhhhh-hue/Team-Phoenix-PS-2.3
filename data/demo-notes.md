# Cell Energy: Study Notes

## PHOTOSYNTHESIS
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into stored chemical energy.
Chlorophyll is a green pigment that absorbs light used in photosynthesis.
Chloroplasts are the organelles where photosynthesis occurs in plant cells.
The raw materials for photosynthesis are carbon dioxide and water. The products include glucose and oxygen.
Light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.

## CELLULAR RESPIRATION
Cellular respiration releases usable energy from glucose.
Aerobic respiration requires oxygen and produces carbon dioxide, water and ATP.
Mitochondria are the main sites of aerobic respiration in eukaryotic cells.
Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and breaks glucose into smaller molecules.
ATP is a molecule that transfers usable energy to many cellular processes.
Photosynthesis stores energy while cellular respiration releases energy that cells can use.

## TRANSPORT ACROSS MEMBRANES
Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.
Osmosis is the net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane toward the region with lower water potential.
Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient and requires cellular energy.
A selectively permeable membrane allows some substances to cross more easily than others.
Diffusion and osmosis are passive processes and do not require a direct input of ATP.
